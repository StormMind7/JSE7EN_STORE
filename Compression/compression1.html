Compression
Bible

Every knob, every instrument. Threshold to Dry/Wet — nothing left out.

Drums need transient control + punch preservation. The goal is to shape the hit without killing the snap. Kick and snare are treated separately — they have entirely different frequency centres and dynamic needs.
🦵
Kick Drum
PUNCH MODE
▾
Algorithm
Classic
Classic mode gives the most transparent, punchy response — great for kick transients.
Threshold
−18 dBFS
Set so only peaks trigger compression. The transient snap of the beater stays untouched.
Ratio
4:1
Firm but not squashed. Tames the body without destroying impact.
Attack
30 ms
Slow attack lets the initial transient click pass through before compression kicks in.
Release
80 ms
Fast enough to recover before the next hit. Keeps the groove breathing.
Knee
Medium
Smooth transition into compression — avoids hard clamping on the low-end thump.
Lookahead
1.5 ms
Helps the compressor anticipate the hit. Reduces clipping on hard transients.
Gain (Output)
+3 dB
Make-up gain to restore loudness after gain reduction. Match the uncompressed level.
Dry/Wet
80 %
Parallel blend. 20% dry signal reintroduces natural transient snap if overcompressed.
Gain Reduction
−4 to −6 dB
Target. Watch the GR meter — any more than −8dB starts to kill the punch.
💡 Pro Tip
Enable the SC EQ (Sidechain EQ) button inside Pro-C2. High-pass the sidechain at ~80Hz so subsonic rumble doesn't trigger false compression. Also try the Hold knob at 20ms to lock the compressor open slightly after each hit — this creates a bigger "bloom" on the body of the kick.

Snare Drum
SNAP MODE
▾
Algorithm
Clean
Clean mode is linear-phase and precise — perfect for a modern snare that needs clarity.
Threshold
−14 dBFS
Snares hit hard. Set threshold so main hits compress but ghost notes pass through.
Ratio
5:1
Keeps hard hits controlled and consistent in the mix.
Attack
8 ms
Faster attack than kick — snare's "crack" is in the first milliseconds. Don't be too slow.
Release
120 ms
Longer release lets the snare tail breathe and ring naturally.
Knee
Hard–Medium
Tighter knee brings that modern punchy snare feel.
Lookahead
0.5 ms
Very small — just enough to catch the transient peak cleanly.
Gain (Output)
+4 dB
Push back the compressed signal to sit in the mix properly.
SC High-Pass
200 Hz
Filter the sidechain so low-end bleed from kick doesn't trigger the snare compressor.
Dry/Wet
100 %
Full wet for snare — you want complete control over the dynamics.
💡 Pro Tip
Try Pro-C2's Opto algorithm for a vintage snare. The Opto mode has a nonlinear release that sounds organic — great for live drum recordings. Also, set the Stereo Link to around 60–70% if you're compressing a stereo snare (with room mic blended). This prevents the image from narrowing too much on loud hits.

Hi-Hat
AIR MODE
▾
Algorithm
Clean
Transparent. You don't want coloration on hats — they live in the high-frequency air.
Threshold
−10 dBFS
High threshold — only tame the loudest open hi-hat hits. Let closed hats be free.
Ratio
2:1
Very gentle. Hi-hats are dynamic by nature — over-compressing kills their shimmer.
Attack
5 ms
Quick enough to catch the metallic edge but not so fast it eats the sparkle.
Release
60 ms
Short release keeps the groove tight. Don't let it linger.
Knee
Soft
Gradual knee makes compression nearly invisible — which is exactly what you want.
Gain (Output)
+1.5 dB
Minimal make-up. Hi-hats shouldn't be loud — just consistent.
SC High-Pass
5 kHz
Extreme HPF on sidechain — only the high-end of the hat itself triggers compression.
Stereo Link
100 %
Full stereo link. Keeps panned hats balanced.
💡 Pro Tip
For hi-hats, less is more. The main goal of the compressor here is evening out dynamics between open and closed patterns — not squashing. Use the GR meter to make sure you're seeing no more than −2 to −3dB of gain reduction. Anything more will make hats sound dull and lifeless.
Precision percussion (shakers, claps, tambourine, congas, etc.) needs tight transient shaping. The goal is consistency across a performance — each hit should feel equally weighted in the mix.
🪘
Precision Percussion
TRANSIENT MODE
▾
Algorithm
Classic
Classic has the most musical knee behaviour — good for live percussion with real dynamics.
Threshold
−16 dBFS
Medium threshold to catch velocity inconsistencies while preserving feel.
Ratio
3:1
Moderate ratio — tightens without robotics. Percussion needs to feel human.
Attack
12 ms
Medium-fast. Let the click of the hit pass, then compress the body.
Release
Auto
Use Auto Release — Pro-C2's algorithm adapts to the tempo of the hit pattern intelligently.
Knee
Medium
Balanced knee for a natural, musical compression curve.
Hold
10 ms
Brief hold prevents pumping between fast hits like shaker 16ths.
Lookahead
1 ms
Small lookahead to catch sharp transients before they clip.
Gain (Output)
+3 dB
Push compressed signal back up to desired level in mix.
Dry/Wet
90 %
Mostly wet but a touch of dry helps retain performance dynamics.
💡 Pro Tip
For shakers and tambourines, drop the ratio to 2:1 and use a soft knee — these instruments need their jingle to breathe. For congas and bongos, you can push to 4:1 with a faster attack (5ms) to tighten up hand performance inconsistencies. Always check in context of the full mix — percussion compressors that sound great solo often disappear in a mix.
Guitar compression is about sustain, presence, and consistency. Rhythm guitar needs glue and even strumming. Lead guitar needs sustain and sitting in the mix. These are very different compression tasks.
🎸
Acoustic / Rhythm Guitar
GLUE MODE
▾
Algorithm
Opto
Opto's nonlinear release sounds warm and musical on acoustic guitars — vintage vibe.
Threshold
−20 dBFS
Lower threshold catches the full strumming pattern for even dynamics.
Ratio
3:1
Gentle ratio — acoustic guitar has natural expression that you want to preserve.
Attack
25 ms
Lets the pick attack come through before compression — keeps the strumming bright.
Release
Auto
Auto-release adapts to tempo. Prevents pumping on strummed chord patterns.
Knee
Soft
Very gradual knee. Acoustic guitar needs compression to be invisible.
Gain (Output)
+2.5 dB
Modest make-up. Keep guitar from sitting too loud in the mix.
Stereo Link
80 %
High stereo link for stereo-mic'd acoustic — maintains the natural width.
Dry/Wet
75 %
Parallel blend. 25% dry restores pick dynamics that compression may dull.
💡 Pro Tip
For electric rhythm guitar, switch to the Classic algorithm and push ratio to 4:1 with a faster attack (10ms). This glues hard strums. For lead guitar, use Vocal algorithm at 2:1 with a slow attack (40ms) and long release (300ms) — this fattens sustain without clamping the notes. Use SC EQ to cut around 250Hz so boxy low-mids don't over-trigger the compressor.
Strings are the most dynamically complex instruments in an arrangement. Violin, viola, cello — each has a vastly different frequency range and dynamic envelope. The goal is transparency: compression should never be heard, only felt.
🎻
Violin
AIR & PRESENCE
▾
Algorithm
Clean
Linear phase. Violin's upper harmonics are delicate — Clean preserves them perfectly.
Threshold
−24 dBFS
Low threshold for consistent engagement. Violin performances have extreme dynamic range.
Ratio
1.5:1
Very light ratio. Barely touching it. The goal is levelling, not squashing.
Attack
50 ms
Slow attack preserves the bow attack (articulation) of each note.
Release
300 ms
Long release follows the natural sustain envelope of bowed strings.
Knee
Very Soft
The softest knee makes the compression curve nearly invisible.
Lookahead
3 ms
Gives the compressor time to react smoothly to bow changes and dynamic swells.
Gain (Output)
+2 dB
Light make-up gain — strings usually don't need to be pushed hard.
Dry/Wet
60 %
40% dry blend maintains the natural expression and air of the performance.
💡 Pro Tip
For solo violin, use the Vocal algorithm instead — it handles complex harmonic content very musically. With an ensemble violin section, compress the bus lightly to "glue" the section: ratio 2:1, slow attack 60ms, medium release 200ms, soft knee. The SC EQ notch around 2–4kHz can stop harsh bow noise from over-triggering.

80 Hz

Orchestral instruments and flute are about dynamic shaping without dynamics removal. An orchestra's power comes from its dynamic range — compression here is surgical and subtle. Bus compression is often more appropriate than individual channel compression.
🎼
Orchestra Bus
GLUE & CONTROL
▾
Algorithm
Bus
Bus algorithm is purpose-built for mix bus glue. Perfect for stitching orchestral sections.
Threshold
−18 dBFS
Set so the compressor breathes with the orchestra — engaged during fortissimo, released during pianissimo.
Ratio
1.5:1
Ultra-gentle. Orchestra dynamics are precious — don't squash them.
Attack
80 ms
Very slow — lets orchestral swells and crescendos develop naturally before limiting them.
Release
500 ms
Long, smooth release follows the decay of orchestral phrases and reverb tails.
Knee
Very Soft
Maximum softness. The transition into compression must be completely imperceptible.
Lookahead
5 ms
Gives the compressor time to anticipate forte passages and engage gently ahead of them.
Stereo Link
100 %
Full link. Orchestra panning must remain stable — unlinked compression shifts the image.
Gain (Output)
+1 dB
Minimal make-up. Orchestra shouldn't be artificially loud — maintain relative dynamics.
GR Target
−1 to −2 dB
Only 1–2dB of gain reduction. Any more destroys the illusion of a live orchestra.
💡 Pro Tip
For orchestral production, consider using Pro-C2 in M/S (Mid-Side) mode. Compress the Mid channel slightly more than the Sides — this tightens the centre (where the conductor's section sits) while leaving the outer orchestral spread (Side) completely natural and airy. Ratio: Mid 2:1 / Side 1.2:1.

Release
250 ms
Long enough to follow phrase tails without pumping between notes.
Knee
Soft
Soft knee — transparency is critical for delicate flute tone.

300 Hz
High-pass sidechain to prevent room noise and breath pops from triggering compression.

Synth saws are harmonically dense and sustain-heavy. They don't have transients like drums — they have density and harmonic texture. Compression here is about controlling harmonic energy, managing low-end power (Low Saws), balancing presence (Mid Saws), and controlling harshness (High Saws).
🔴

−12 dBFS
High threshold — only engage on the brightest, most piercing moments.

High saws often have resonant filter sweeps. These create sudden volume spikes as the cutoff passes through resonant frequencies. SUB CONTROL

Classic handles low-frequency content well — accurate GR with no phase issues on subs.

−20 dBFS
Low saws are powerful — set threshold so compression is almost always active.

4:1 to 6:1
Firm compression. Low saws can overwhelm mixes — keep them tight and consistent.
Attack
20 ms
Moderate. Low frequencies are slow cycles — don't attack faster than the waveform itself.
Release
250 ms
Medium-long. Low saws hold their energy — release must match their sustain length.

Medium-Hard
Tighter knee to get a firm handle on sub-bass energy. Control over musicality here.
Lookahead
2 ms
Essential for low frequencies — they need extra time for the compressor to react cleanly.

40 Hz
Remove sub-30Hz infrasound from the sidechain so inaudible vibrations don't distort GR.

+4 dB
More make-up needed for aggressive compression — restore the low-end weight.

90 %
High wet for control. Low-end uncontrolled in a mix causes everything else to suffer.

Low saws are often sidechained to the kick drum (external sidechain) rather than self-compressed — this creates the classic "ducking" movement where the kick punches through the bass layer. In Pro-C2, enable Ext. SC and route kick to the sidechain input. Set attack 5ms, release 150ms — this creates breathing room for the kick in the low-end every beat.

Complete Pro-C2 Knob Reference
ALL CONTROLS
▾
Threshold
Sets the level above which compression begins
Lower = more compression triggered. Higher = only loudest peaks compressed. This is where you tell the compressor "start working here."
Ratio
How hard compression is applied
2:1 = gentle levelling. 4:1 = firm control. 10:1+ = limiting. ∞:1 = hard brick wall. Higher ratio = more squash.
Algorithm
The character of the compressor
Classic (transparent punch), Clean (linear-phase), Opto (vintage, nonlinear), Bus (glue), Vocal (breath-tracking), Mastering (precision), Safe (lookahead limiting).
Attack
How fast compression kicks in
Fast (1–10ms) = catches transients, can sound squashed. Slow (30–100ms) = lets transients through, preserves punch. Attack shapes the "hit" feeling.
Release
How fast compression lets go
Too fast = pumping/breathing. Too slow = compressor stays engaged, squashes everything. Match to the tempo and sustain of the source.
Hold
Delay before release begins
Keeps compressor "clamped" briefly after the signal drops below threshold. Prevents chattering on drums. Often overlooked but very powerful.
Knee
Softness of compression transition
Hard knee = abrupt, precise. Soft knee = gradual, musical. For transparent compression, always use soft. For drum punch, use medium or hard.
Lookahead
Pre-read the incoming signal
Lets the compressor "see" peaks before they hit and prepare. Adds latency but prevents transient clipping. Essential for limiting. 0–5ms range.
SC EQ (Sidechain EQ)
Frequency-specific compression triggering
Boost a frequency to make compressor more sensitive there. Cut a frequency to ignore it. This is how you do "de-essing", kick-sensitive sidechaining, and frequency-focused compression.
Gain (Output)
Make-up gain after compression
Compression reduces level. Output gain restores it. Match compressed output level to bypass level for honest A/B comparison. Louder always sounds better — compensate before judging.
Dry/Wet (Mix)
Parallel compression blend
100% = full compression. 50% = half compressed + half original. This is built-in parallel compression. Use below 100% to retain natural dynamics while still adding density.
Stereo Link
L/R channel coupling
100% = both channels compress equally (stable stereo image). 0% = each channel independent (can shift stereo field). For most sources, 70–100% is correct.
Gain Reduction Meter
How much compression is being applied
The most important visual feedback tool. Watch this constantly. −2 to −4dB = gentle glue. −6 to −10dB = firm control. Beyond −10dB = aggressive limiting territory.
External SC
Trigger compression with a different signal
Route kick drum to compress bass. Route a reference track to compress effects. The compressor listens to the sidechain signal but processes the main signal.
SC Listen
Audition the sidechain signal
Not a processing knob — a diagnostic tool. Enable it to hear exactly what the compressor is "hearing." Use when setting SC EQ to make sure you're filtering correctly.
